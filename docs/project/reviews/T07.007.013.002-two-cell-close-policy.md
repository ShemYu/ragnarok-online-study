## Core correction (source)

- `_Ledger` (private, one per `write_candidate` call). Each successful open becomes an
  `_Acquisition` with a monotonic token (1..6) and a fixed role: source, directory,
  binary_write, binary_readback, receipt_write, receipt_readback. It is registered
  immediately after `_open` returns. `close(rec)` sets `closing` with
  `close_attempts=1` before the one `_close`. A normal return gives `closed`. Any
  ordinary `Exception` gives `uncertain`, appends `CloseError` and re-raises the same
  object. A second close of any record is refused (`ledger_close_state`) without
  calling `_close`. `cleanup()` walks records in reverse and closes only `open` ones.
  It continues after failures and skips closing/closed/uncertain records. It does
  no errno/fstat/inode inference, dup, finalizer or retry.
- `CloseError(acquisition, role, "close", error, "unknown")` and
  `Ownership(acquisition, role, state, close_attempts)` are immutable tuple
  subclasses (`__slots__=()`, read-only properties). Ownership holds no descriptor.
- `CandidateIOError(CandidateError)` carries `code`, `primary_error` (the exact first
  failure, also set as `__cause__`), `close_errors` (a tuple, in occurrence order)
  and `ownership` (a tuple of at most 6 records). If the primary is a
  `CandidateError`, the code is its code, so all existing refusal codes are kept.
  Otherwise it is `<stage>_close` when the primary is itself a close error, or
  `<stage>_error`, where stage is source, transform or output. A close-first
  failure is both the primary and a `close_errors` member.
- `write_candidate` runs `_write_attempt` once. It returns only when no close
  errors were recorded and every record is `closed` by the normal path, checked
  before cleanup. After any failure it runs cleanup exactly once, then raises.
  Before any acquisition it re-raises the original exception, so old
  pre-acquisition refusals and `TypeError` are unchanged. After acquisition it
  raises `CandidateIOError` from the primary. Non-`Exception` BaseExceptions are
  re-raised after cleanup without wrapping.
- The removed `_close_owned` and per-step `fds.remove()` are what lost
  acquisitions and dropped errors in .001. No `finally` block remains that could
  shadow an error.
- Writer I/O is otherwise the same: read-only admission, bounded EOF/hash/identity
  checks, exclusive NOFOLLOW 0700/0600 output, readback, final audit, two-file
  receipt. Failures preserve source, partial and foreign artifacts. No atomicity
  or rollback is claimed.

## Limitations

The ledger provides truthfulness and protection against foreign closes. It does
not provide same-process release. Release after an uncertain close depends on
ordinary OS teardown of an externally observed, retired, non-delegated writer
process, and this report does not establish that. No durability, rollback or
two-file atomicity is claimed. Fixture-stub receipts are writer-unit evidence
only. Before-close faults are simulated by hooks: an imitation cannot prove how
a real `close(2)` fails.
